How Kalki-Kit works
  1. 1
    You paste a claim.

    A headline, a forwarded message or a statement. Check one claim at a time for the best result.

  2. 2
    Smart searching.

    An AI model works out what to look up: key names, places and numbers, with spelling corrected.

  3. 3
    Evidence is gathered.

    Live news headlines, published fact-checks (Alt News, BOOM, Factly, Snopes and others) and Wikipedia are searched at the same time.

  4. 4
    The AI judges only that evidence.

    It is told not to rely on memory. A claim is only marked true or false if at least one source found directly says so. Otherwise the result is Can't verify.

News link

Paste an article address. We read it, find the main claim it makes and check that claim. We also flag satire and sensational wording.

Image check

Upload or paste a photo or screenshot. We look for visible signs of AI generation or editing, read any text in it, check any claim that comes with it, and show you how to find where it first appeared.

Speak a claim

In Chrome and Edge you can say a claim in English, Hindi or Marathi instead of typing it.

Before you share anything
  • Pause if it makes you angry or scared. That is how false posts spread.
  • Check the source: who published it, and can you find the same story on a trusted site?
  • Check the date: old photos and stories are often shared as if they were new.
  • Look at the whole story, not just the headline or the picture.
Good to know
  • Results are AI-assisted. For important decisions, open the linked sources and read them yourself.
  • Very recent events may not be reported yet, which gives a Can't verify result rather than a guess.
  • Checks are saved only in this browser (My Checks). Images you upload are sent to Google's AI to be analysed and are not stored on our server.
  • For images, "no signs found" never proves a picture is real, and "AI-generated" never proves the story is false.
